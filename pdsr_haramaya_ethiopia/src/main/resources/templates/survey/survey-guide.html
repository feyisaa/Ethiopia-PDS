1. Purpose and guidance for completing the tool

This tool helps facility teams, subnational managers, and partners understand how well MPDSR, MDSR, or PDSR is being implemented at the facility level. Its purpose is to highlight what is working well and where there are gaps, so the team can focus efforts on strengthening the MPDSR process. It highlights the enabling factors or inputs needed to improve MPDSR implementation at the facility level

What it is not meant for: Assessing the quality of MPDSR tools and processes. Other tools have been developed for assessing clinical data quality for MPDSR.

  • What is the format? Assessing the quality of MPDSR tools and processes. Other tools have been developed for assessing clinical data quality for MPDSR.
  • How often is it completed? It is recommended that it is applied at least quarterly in alignment with supervision meetings.
  • Who completes it? It should be completed by the team involved in MPDSR, led by the focal person or coordinator for MPDSR at the facility, or any designated person. It will draw on recent meetings and documentation. After completion, it should be reviewed by someone else—such as the facility manager, the quality improvement team, or a district representative—to confirm the results. If the scorer and reviewer disagree, they should discuss the evidence and agree on a final answer. The second person must "validate" the score on the eMPDSR system.
  • How to complete it? The tool contains 30 yes/no questions, each reflecting one element of good implementation linked to 10 categories. A “yes” should only be selected when the facility can fully demonstrate that the requirement is in place. Partial or intermediate responses are not options because the objective is to determine which implementation factors require improvement. There is an option to write a comment to explain your answer. Each “yes” gives one point, for a total possible score of 30.

    This Tool Guide provides detailed guidance for each question to ensure consistent scoring. It includes information on how to collect the information, such as review MPDSR meeting minutes, death review tools, or other official documents.

2. Survey Questions
Category 1. Leadership & Accountability

Q1. Is there a designated person responsible for coordinating MPDSR activities at your facility (MPDSR Focal Person)?
Guidance: Confirm through meeting minutes, formal designation documents, or staff interview.
Score “Yes” only if a specific individual is formally assigned responsibility for coordinating MPDSR activities.

Q2. Did the facility manager or their representative participate in at least 50% of MPDSR meetings at the facility during the assessment period?
Guidance: Check attendance records (≥50% participation).

Q3. In the last quarter, has the facility received a district-level monitoring and supervision visit to support implementation of MPDSR activities?
Guidance: Review supervision reports, visitors’ books, or written correspondence.
Score "Yes" only if support occurred at least quarterly.

Category 2. Policy & Guidelines

Q4. Are MPDSR guidelines or SOPs available and accessible to staff involved in MPDSR (national or local SOP)?
Guidance: Ask the staff to show the document to confirm accessibility.
Score "Yes" only if the guideline/SOP is accessible (paper or electronic)..
Example guidelines/SOPs include:

  • National MPDSR implementation guidelines/policies
  • MPDSR identification and notification SOP
  • Death review and meeting SOP
  • Action tracking and follow-up SOP
  • Confidentiality and no-blame guideline
  • MPDSR documentation and reporting protocol
Category 3. Structure & Coordination

Q5. Is there a formal MPDSR committee established for maternal deaths?
Guidance: Confirm existence through formal records or meeting minutes.

Q6. Is there a formal MPDSR committee established for perinatal deaths?
Guidance: Confirm through formal documentation or meeting minutes. It can be same committee as maternal deaths.

Q7. Do the maternal and/or perinatal death review meetings include representatives from different required health cadres, as per the national MPDSR guideline?
Guidance: Review attendance lists in meeting minutes.
Score "Yes" only if representation aligns with required cadres specified in national MPDSR guidelines.

Q8. Does the MPDSR committee have formal Terms of Reference (TOR) or a code of conduct with assigned roles?
Guidance: Review MPDSR files.
Score "Yes" only if a written Terms of Reference (TOR) or code of conduct exists and clearly defines roles and responsibilities.

Category 4. Review Meetings

Q9. Does the MPDSR committee meet to review deaths?
Guidance: Review meeting schedules or minutes.
Score "Yes" only if at least one documented meeting was held to review deaths during the assessment period.

Q10. Are MPDSR review meetings held regularly (e.g., monthly or as per national guidelines)?
Guidance: Review meeting calendars or sets of minutes.
Score "Yes" only if meetings were held according to the frequency specified in national guidelines (e.g., monthly).

Category 5. Tools

Q11. Are all required maternal death tools (notification, review) available based on the most recent national guideline?
Guidance: Verify paper or electronic availability.

Q12. Is there some evidence that the maternal death review tool is being used?
Guidance: Review completed forms or electronic records.
Score "Yes" only if at least one maternal death review tool has been completed during the assessment period (at least partly completed). No maternal death, indicate not applicable

Q13. Are all of the maternal death tools fully completed?
Guidance: Conduct a sample of all tools and check if the tools are fully completed (paper-based and electronic) and accurately entered from paper-based to electronic, if relevant.
Score "Yes" only if sampled maternal death tools are all fully and correctly completed according to national standards. If no maternal death, indicate not applicable.

Q14. Are all required perinatal death tools (notification, review) available based on the most recent national guideline?
Guidance: Verify paper or electronic availability.

Q15. Is there some evidence that the perinatal death review tool is being used?
Guidance: Review completed forms or electronic records.
Score "Yes" only if at least one perinatal death review tool has been completed during the assessment period (at least partly completed). If no perinatal death, indicate not applicable.

Q16. Are all of the perinatal death tools fully completed?
Guidance: Conduct a random sample of all tools (10% or minimum of 3, whichever is greater) and check if the tools are fully completed (paper-based and electronic) and if the tools aligned.
Score "Yes" only if sampled perinatal death tools are all fully and correctly completed according to national standards. If no perinatal death, indicate not applicable.

Category 6. Training & Staff Capacity

Q17. Has someone at the facility been trained in any aspect of MPDSR?
Guidance: Review training logs or confirm through staff interview
Score "Yes" only if at least one staff member has received any MPDSR-related training, mentorship, or information sharing. It did not need to be formal training and could have happened at any time.

Q18. Have at least 50% of MPDSR committee members completed any formal training on MPDSR (including e-learning, online, face to face)?
Guidance: Check training logs/databases or training certificates/reports.
Score "Yes" only if at least 50% of MPDSR committee member completed formal MPDSR training (onsite, offsite, or online, This includes e-learning courses).

Q19. Are MPDSR related activities, such as meeting attendance, included in the staff performance appraisal process for MPDSR committee members?
Guidance: Confirm with HR or facility manager and review appraisal documentation.
Score "Yes" only if MPDSR participation is formally included in staff performance appraisal processes.

Category 7. Resources

Q20. Does the facility use some of its budget or resources to support the implementation of MPDSR?
Guidance: Ask facility manager if they support MPDSR related processes. This may include photocopying forms, stationary, tea and refreshments, etc...
Score "Yes" only if facility funds or in-kind resources have been allocated to support MPDSR implementation.

Q21. Does the facility attempt to mobilize or access resources to support the implementation of MPDSR recommendations?
Guidance: Ask facility manager and validate with another MPDSR committee member. Look for evidence of budgets that respond to the recommendations and documentation that show the attempts of managers to advocate for additional resources.
Score "Yes" only if the facility can demonstrate that it has tried to mobilize or access resources to support MPDSR recommendations.

Q22. Has the facility spent funds or resources to act on MPDSR recommendations?
Guidance: Review meeting minutes, financial records, or implementation reports.
Score "Yes" only if documented funds or resources were used to implement at least one MPDSR recommendation.

Category 8. Community Engagement

Q23. Is there a system or structure (i.e. group) that facilitates communication between the facility/health system and the community on health issues, and does the MPDSR committee have a designated representative responsible for engaging this structure?
Guidance: Check if there is an existing structure by looking at community engagement reports or records of community engagement. Check if someone is responsible in the MPDSR committee for taking forward community level recommendation with community structure(s).
Score "Yes" only if a structure exists to facilitate facility–community communication and if a representative of the MPDSR committee is responsible for providing feedback to this structure.

Community structures may include:

  • Community or village health committees
  • Women and Youth Groups
  • Unit committees or area councils
  • Community leaders (hiefs, queen mothers, religious leaders and elders)

Q24. Have MPDSR recommendations from the last quarter been discussed or shared with community structures?
Guidance: Verify through meeting reports or documentation.
Score "Yes" only if MPDSR recommendations have been formally shared or discussed with community structures.

Category 9. Implementation Culture

Q25. Are participants reminded about the purpose of MPDSR during the review meetings (learning and improvement, not blame)?
Guidance: Review meeting agendas or minutes.
Score "Yes" only if documentation explicitly states that the purpose of MPDSR is learning and improvement rather than blame

Q26. Do meeting minutes maintain confidentiality of staff and patients when discussing specific patient cases?
Guidance: Check that names of patients and the staff providing care are not included in meeting minutes when discussing specific cases.
Score "Yes" only if patient and staff names are not recorded when discussing specific cases.

Q27. In the last quarter, were the training needs identified through the MPDSR meetings implemented?
Guidance: Review meeting minutes or agendas.
Score "Yes" only if documentation shows that training needs (relating to improving quality of maternal and newborn care) were identified during recent meetings, and these were followed up with a related training.

Category 10. Tracking Response

Q28. Does the facility use an action tracker to monitor implementation of recommendations?
Guidance: Verify by seeing tracker. This may be an action tracker through eMPDSR or could be another format

Q29. Has the facility implemented at least (≥) 50% of MPDSR recommendations from the last quarter?
Guidance: Verify through meeting minutes or e-MPDSR data. A minimum of 50% is needed for a facility to bear the status of “routine integration” of MPDSR which is a step higher than early implementation.

Q30. Has the facility implemented at least (≥) 75% or more of MPDSR recommendations from the last quarter?
Guidance: Verify through meeting minutes or e-MPDSR data. A minimum of 75% is needed to align with the status of MPDSR institutionalisation, which is a step higher than routine integration.

Additional Questions

Q31. How many maternal deaths have been reported to DHIS2 since the last assessment?
Guidance: Look at the monthly reporting forms to extract and calculate how many deaths were reported to DHIS2 since the last assessment.

Q32. How many perinatal deaths have been reported to DHIS2 since the last assessment?
Guidance: Look at the monthly reporting forms to extract and calculate how many deaths were reported to DHIS2 since the last assessment.

3. Synthesis of Assessment Findings: From Scores to Action

Purpose: This section guides you in moving from a simple collection of scores to a meaningful analysis of systemic gaps and the development of a prioritized action plan. The goal is to answer the critical question:
"Given our scores, what should we do next to strengthen our MPDSR system?"

Step 1: Calculate and Visualize the Total Score

Each factor will have received a point or not based on the answer from the survey. Sum all points from the monitoring tool. The total score out of 30 provides a quick snapshot of overall system strength.

A summary of assessment scores:

  • 0-10 Points (Early Stages): The foundational enabling factors of MPDSR are not yet in place. Focus on establishing basic structures and processes.
  • 11-20 Points (Developing Consistency): Foundational enabling factors exist and there is demonstration of implementation. However, not all activities may be in place or activities may be irregular or fragile. Focus on standardizing practices and ensure regular practice.
  • 21-30 Points (Towards Institutionalization): The system is functional, with most enabling factors actively strengthening the cycle. Focus on sustaining high performance, closing the feedback loop, and fostering a culture of continuous learning.
Step 2: Analyze the Score Profile to Diagnose Systemic Gaps

Do not just look at the total score; analyze the pattern. Low scores across multiple factors often point to underlying systemic issues. For example, low leadership and follow-up scores usually mean managers are not really driving the process, so MPDSR ends up feeling like a side task rather than part of improving care. When tools, training, and review meetings score low, it often means MPDSR has slipped into “paperwork mode” instead of helping teams learn from deaths. Low community and culture scores suggest the system is too focused on the facility and that people may not feel comfortable speaking openly about problems. When structure and participation scores are low, it usually points to hierarchy—people in the room, but not everyone feeling able to speak or contribute. Overall, the pattern of low scores helps you see whether the issue is leadership, learning, safety, or teamwork, and where support is needed to strengthen the system.

Step 3: Prioritize Actions for Strengthening MPDSR

Not all gaps can be fixed at once. Use the following criteria to prioritize:

  1. Foundational First: Address enabling factors that are prerequisites for others. You cannot have a good review meeting (Factor 4) without a functioning committee (Factor 3) and the right tools (Factor 5). You cannot track implementation of recommendations easily without use of an action tracker (Factor 10). Some responses cannot be implemented (Factor 10) without allocation of resources (Factor 7). Prioritize foundational enabling factors.
  2. Focus on "Quick Wins": Identify enabling factors that can be improved with minimal resources. For example, formally designating a focal person (Factor 1.1) can be done quickly and can build momentum. A multidisciplinary MPDSR committee can be set up and confidentiality of MPDSR process/minutes can be ensured.
  3. Target the "Response" Loop: The ultimate goal of MPDSR is to prevent future deaths. Prioritize actions that directly close the loop, such as securing a flexible budget for recommendations (Factor 7) and improving the quality of recommendations in tools (Factor 5).
  4. Leverage Strengths: Use your high-scoring enabling factors to address weaknesses. For example, if you have strong Leadership (Factor 1), task them with championing the allocation of resources (Factor 7). If you have a strong multidisciplinary committee (Factor 3), use them to improve the Implementation Culture (Factor 9).
Step 4: Develop a Concrete Action Plan

For each priority gap, create a SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) action.

  • Example: Instead of "Improve leadership involvement."
  • SMART Action: "The Medical Superintendent will attend and co-chair the next three monthly MPDSR meetings, as evidenced by meeting minutes. The focal person will send a personal invitation and agenda one week in advance. (Owner: Focal Person; Timeline: Next 3 months)."
